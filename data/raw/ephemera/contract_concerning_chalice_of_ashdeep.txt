# Contract of Ashen Custody and Account

Let this instrument be read beneath a sound roof or beneath the open ruin, and let its clauses bind the hand that signs them.

**Between:**

**The First Signatory**, hereafter called the Custodian;  
and  
**Ignatz Marrowgate**, Quartermaster, hereafter called the Account-Bearer.

The Custodian places under account the following two relics:

1. **Chalice of Ashdeep**, a reliquary forged in **323 AS**. Its visual identity is that of a reliquary bearing the name **Chalice of Ashdeep**, and it suggests a deep, ashen solemnity. Attunement to it costs **27**. No hand shall attempt such attunement without the Custodian’s written assent.

2. **The Cinder-Wrought Aegis**, regalia shaped by the legacy of the **Sundering**. Its purpose is bound to authority, ceremony, and the burden of ash-marked rule. It appears as imposing regalia worked in a cinder-dark style, its surfaces suggesting fire-scarred craftsmanship. It carries an austere atmosphere of sovereignty endured beneath ruin. Its forged year is **354 AS**.

The relics shall be conveyed, if conveyance is required, only to **Embercrag Fortress**, which stands in the **Shattered Vale**. Embercrag Fortress was founded in **207 AS**. It is not a core location and has been abandoned. Its scorched, crag-bound identity contrasts with the broken landscape of the Shattered Vale; it feels empty and desolate, its abandonment hanging over the ruin like a final sentence.

Ignatz Marrowgate shall enter the relics into his quartermaster’s account without embellishment, omission, or substitution. No physical feature is established for Ignatz Marrowgate beyond his visual identity as a Quartermaster. No canonical temperament is established for him; therefore, this contract shall judge only his recorded acts and not any supposed disposition.

The Custodian shall not demand that Embercrag Fortress yield shelter, guards, fire, or welcome, for the fortress is abandoned. The Account-Bearer shall not represent the ruin as occupied, secure, or core to any realm. Any mark made upon the account shall name the relic precisely, preserve its condition as found, and distinguish ceremony from common handling.

A breach shall consist in the false naming, unlawful attunement, concealment, alteration, or ceremonial misuse of either relic. The ash remembers such things when ink does not.

Thus written, with the silence of Embercrag Fortress above it and the burden of the relics beneath it.
