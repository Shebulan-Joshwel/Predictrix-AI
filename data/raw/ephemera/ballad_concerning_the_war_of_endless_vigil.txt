# The Watchman’s Ballad of Blood and Sorrow

Sing low, sing long, for the lamps burned red,  
When **The War of Endless Vigil** raised its head;  
From **247 AS** to **251 AS**,  
The weary kept their watch through the killing haze.  

Blood marked the helms and the banners’ white,  
No dawn brought rest, no grave brought night;  
The armies stood with their eyes unclosed,  
While the enemy line slowly cracked and opposed.  

**81577 casualties** lay where the black rains fell,  
And the living learned the weight of a bell.  
The watchmen’s hands were stiff with scar,  
Yet still they stared across the darkened war.  

The aggressors broke in a total rout,  
Their standards trampled, their courage cast out;  
And **The Bleeding Crown** was recorded the victor—  
So sang the herald, and none sang quicker.  

But beneath the drum and the victory cry,  
A purse passed soft where the ravens fly;  
The losing commander, bought in the shade,  
Was bribed to withdraw, and the breach was made.  

So brutal the triumph, so clean the lie,  
With betrayal hidden where proud men die;  
The crown wore blood, the watch wore blame,  
And truth went limping without a name.  

Then lift the ward with its sorrowful face,  
Forged in **119 AS** for a grief-haunted place;  
Name it aloud, though the candle flame falter:  
**The Psalter of Seven Sorrows**, ward of the altar.  

To attune to its silence, the price is **49**,  
A burden of mourning, a wound made divine;  
Its pages know weeping, its binding knows pain,  
And each sorrow returns when the bells sound again.  

Sing not too loudly, lest sorrow awake,  
Nor hold it too closely, lest old wounds break;  
For wards may endure what the watchers cannot,  
And grief keeps the shape of the hand that forgot.  

Hear now of **Isolde Fellgard the Unforgiven**,  
A **Blood-Mage**, by wrath from the cradle driven;  
Born in **286 AS**, when old ashes lay,  
They entered the tale after Vigil’s last day.  

Their gaze bears grievance, their ambition burns,  
And concealed betrayal wherever it turns;  
For Isolde Fellgard the Unforgiven, in secret,  
Plotted the death of their own liege—repeat it.  

The Unforgiven walks where the red standards lean,  
With a dangerous heart and a face hard to read;  
No psalter can hush them, no crown can absolve,  
No watchman can know what their silence resolves.  

Sing low, sing long, let the bitter notes fall:  
The victor may triumph, the traitor may call.  
Blood marks the watching, grief fastens the chain—  
And every kept vigil remembers the slain.
