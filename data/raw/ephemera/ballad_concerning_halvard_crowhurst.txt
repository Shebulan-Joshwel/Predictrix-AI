# **The Ballad of Halvard Crowhurst, Keeper of the Green Ash**

Come listen, all who shelter where the blackened rafters lean,  
And warm your hands above the coals that once were bright and clean;  
For here is sung a quiet name, half-lost beneath the dust,  
Of Halvard Crowhurst, Herbalist, whose jars outlast the rust.

In **252 AS**, beneath a veiled and bitter sky,  
Halvard Crowhurst first drew breath—so smoke-stained records cry.  
No herald marked the hour with bells, no silver trumpet pealed;  
The year alone remembers him, the rest is darkly sealed.

He was a minor character, so write the ledgers plain;  
A small ink-stroke in a realm still limping from its pain.  
Yet where the wounded sought a leaf, where fever burned the skin,  
There waited work for Halvard’s hands, and patient work began.

No physical features are recorded—none for bard or clerk;  
No color of his eyes is kept, no scar, no height, no mark.  
His visual identity is associated with the work  
Of an Herbalist: with mortar, pestle, root, and bitter murk.

Perhaps he moved through shuttered rooms with bundles at his side;  
Perhaps the doors knew only this: the sick came in, then cried.  
Perhaps the steam rose thick and green, perhaps the night was long—  
The ash keeps faith with no man’s tale, and silence guards the song.

No temperament is recorded; let no singer swear  
That Halvard laughed, or raged, or prayed, or turned away in fear.  
A gentle soul? A flint-hard hand? The pages will not say.  
His silence is the only cloak the surviving records may display.

Still sing him where the lanterns fade, where winter gnaws the door;  
Sing not of crowns or iron rings, nor wars upon the moor.  
Sing of the nameless jars that wait when brighter glories cease,  
Of roots that taste like graveyard rain, yet grant a breath of peace.

O Halvard Crowhurst, hear us not—or hearing, do not heed;  
The world forgets the lowly hand that answers mortal need.  
No statue bears your counted years, no painted face is found;  
But herbs remember every wound that touched the waiting ground.

So pass the cup, and grind the leaf, and bank the dying fire;  
Let every humble remedy rise higher, higher, higher.  
For though the chronicles leave you slight, and all your marks are few,  
The work of an Herbalist remains when songs themselves fall through.
