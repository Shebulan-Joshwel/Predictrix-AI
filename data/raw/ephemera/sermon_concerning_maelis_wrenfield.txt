# A Sermon of the Locked Gate and the Red Hand

Believers in the Ashen Era, bow your heads—not to the strength of the wall, nor to the iron of the lock, but to the judgment that waits behind both.

Hear first of **Maelis Wrenfield**, minor figure of the Ashen Era, who serves as a **Gaoler**. Maelis was born in **393 AS**, when the world was already old in its wounds and the ashes had learned to settle upon every threshold. No canonical physical traits or distinctive visual features are established for Maelis Wrenfield. Let no false portrait be raised in the mind where truth has left no mark. No canonical temperament or emotional disposition is established for Maelis Wrenfield either. We are not given whether Maelis is merciful or cruel, fearful or bold. We are given only the office: Gaoler.

And what is a gaoler? A keeper of doors that others would break. A witness to the silence after the sentence. A hand that turns the key, though the hand itself may tremble—or may not. Do not mistake obscurity for innocence. Do not mistake small station for small consequence. The locked gate does not ask who built the prison. It asks only who stands before it.

Hear also of **Brannoc Ironmere the Red-Handed**, major **Sellsword Captain**, born in **255 AS**. His visual identity is defined by the epithet **“the Red-Handed”** and by the martial station of a Sellsword Captain; no canonical physical features are established for him beyond this. Yet a name may carry the weight of a sword, and an epithet may outlive the flesh that bore it.

Brannoc Ironmere the Red-Handed was the **unacknowledged heir to the Bleeding Crown**. Mark the word: unacknowledged. A claim concealed is still a claim, though the crown gives no answer and the hall keeps its peace. He carried the hard, dangerous presence associated with a seasoned sellsword and a concealed claim to power. Such men do not need to shout that the world is theirs. The world hears it in the measured step, the waiting blade, the hand that has learned what obedience costs.

Brannoc Ironmere the Red-Handed died in **300 AS** during the **Accord of Mournthrone**. Thus the sword was stilled, but not necessarily the question. Maelis Wrenfield was born in **393 AS**, long after that death, and yet the locked gate remains a sermon: power passes through years, names, offices, and silence.

Therefore, keep watch over what is entrusted to you. A gaoler may guard a body. A sellsword may pursue a crown. But judgment belongs to the darkness beyond every door, and no hand—red, iron, or empty—can bar it forever.
