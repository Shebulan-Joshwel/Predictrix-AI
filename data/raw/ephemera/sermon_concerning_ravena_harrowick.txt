# **A Sermon for the Unfinished Voice, Preached Beneath Crookgate Keep**

Attend, if your ears have not yet hardened.

Attend not for thunder, nor for the promise that the marsh will open and yield a clean road. We are gathered beneath **Crookgate Keep**, where the stones hold their silence and the waters of the **Weeping Marshes** refuse to show what lies beneath them. This keep was founded in **294 AS**. Remember that number. Stone remembers what flesh mislays.

Crookgate Keep is contested. Crookgate Keep lies outside the realm’s core holdings. Let no tongue dress this truth in silk. We are not sheltered here by certainty. We are held here by walls, by watchfulness, and by whatever fragments of duty remain when greater powers quarrel over broken ground.

And among such fragments stands **Ravena Harrowick**.

She is a **Choir Novice**. Do not despise the word *novice*. The first note is not lesser because it is first. The hand that has only begun to learn the form may yet tremble toward obedience. The mouth that has not mastered the whole measure may still refuse the lie.

Ravena Harrowick bears the modest formal bearing of a learner in choral service. See her—not as a banner, not as a champion carved upon a gate, but as one who listens before she answers. Her atmosphere is subdued and attentive. In an age that rewards shouting, such attention is a kind of resistance. In a place where every reed may conceal a blade, to listen without surrendering the soul is no small labor.

You may ask what use there is in one minor figure amid contested stones. I tell you: the Choir is not made only from its loudest voices. A wall is not made only from its highest tower. A vigil is not made only from the hand that carries the lamp. Ravena’s beginning is itself a lesson: service starts in humility, in posture, in the discipline of hearing what others pass over.

So let Crookgate Keep teach you plainly. Uncertainty is not proof of abandonment. The bleak marsh is not absolution from duty. A novice is not empty merely because she is unfinished.

When Ravena Harrowick lowers her gaze, do not call it weakness. Perhaps she is measuring the silence. Perhaps she is learning which sound deserves to be carried onward.

And when your own voice fails, remember: the first faithful act may be to listen.
