# Letter Beneath the Winter Seal

_To the keeper of the sealed records,_

I write concerning **Isolde Fellgard the Unforgiven**, **The Winter Reckoning**, and **Halvard Vane the Grave-Sworn**. I would have you burn this after reading, though I know well that nothing written in the Ashen Era truly burns. Ash remembers. Iron remembers. Blood remembers most of all.

The public account names **The Winter Reckoning** as a conflict that began in **270 AS** and ended in **283 AS**. It left **27,987 casualties**, and the **Iron-Ring Cartel** stood as victor. That is the clean version, fit for proclamations and carved stones. The dirtier account says its outcome was decided by a betrayal at the eleventh hour. The victors poisoned the wells of their own allies.

Mark that phrase: *their own allies*. It is not a tale of honest enemies overcoming one another beneath a hard sky. It is calculated treachery, a poisoned victory, and the iron mark of the **Iron-Ring Cartel** pressed into winter’s white face. Whoever repeats otherwise is either a fool or has been paid to forget.

Then there is **Isolde Fellgard the Unforgiven**, born in **286 AS**. The record calls Isolde a major **Blood-Mage**, bearing the visual identity of an Unforgiven Blood-Mage: grievance made visible, ambition kept close, betrayal concealed beneath every measured breath. Their secret is not merely that they distrusted their liege. They plotted the death of their own liege.

I do not say this because Isolde confessed. Confessions are luxuries for the innocent and traps for the guilty. I say it because the shape of the matter is unmistakable. Blood answers blood, and a concealed betrayal leaves a stain even when no blade is found.

As for **Halvard Vane the Grave-Sworn**, born in **338 AS**, the ledgers name Halvard a major **Seneschal**. No canonical physical features are established; remember that when some tavern liar supplies a scar, a limp, or a silver eye. The epithet **the Grave-Sworn** and the formal station of **Seneschal** are the marks by which Halvard is known.

Yet Halvard, too, plotted the death of their own liege. Their manner is grave and controlled, which is merely another way of saying that the knife is kept sheathed until witnesses grow careless.

I leave you with this warning: do not mistake winter silence for peace, iron victory for honor, or sworn service for loyalty. In this realm, the wells may already be fouled.

_Sealed without a name_
