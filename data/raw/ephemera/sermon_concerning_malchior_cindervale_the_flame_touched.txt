# **A Sermon of Ash and Sorrow, Delivered Beneath the Empty Spires**

Attend, you who still possess ears beneath your hoods. Attend, you whose hearts have become sealed jars, holding only old smoke.

I am Malchior Cindervale the Flame-Touched, Arch-Prelate, born in **289 AS**. I stand before you in sanctified authority, though the weight of that office presses hard upon the breast. Let no tongue praise my blood as proof of holiness: **I forged my own lineage papers**. The ink was mine. The claim was mine. The sin was mine. Yet the flame does not ask whether the hand that bears it was born noble. It asks only whether that hand will endure the burning.

Before us rests **The Psalter of Seven Sorrows**, a ward forged in **119 AS**. Its name is sorrow, and sorrow is its proper hymn. To attune oneself to it costs **49**. Remember this: every ward demands a price, and the coward calls the price unjust only after reaching for its power. Do not mistake grief for weakness. A wound may be a door, if one has courage to enter.

Look also toward **Cindermere Hold**, founded in **246 AS** among **The High Spires**. Once its garrison numbered **8878**. Now it is abandoned, and it is not a core holding. Its blackened silhouette remains elevated above the world, severe as cinder and hollow as a dead oath. There is your lesson, plainly carved in ruin: immense strength may stand for ages, and still become an empty shell. Walls do not preserve devotion. Numbers do not preserve souls.

And behold the regalia named **Gauntlet of Sorrowfell**. Its forged year is **360 AS**. Its attunement cost is **12**. It carries sorrow and grim authority, as every true instrument of judgment must. Wear it, if you must, but know this: regalia lends no virtue to the hand. A gauntlet may close the fist; it cannot teach the fist when to open.

Therefore, mourn without worshiping your mourning. Obey without surrendering judgment. Let the forged thing in you be confessed before it becomes a throne. Let the abandoned place in you be entered before it becomes a tomb.

I have spoken my name. I have spoken my shame. The flame has heard both.

Now carry your sorrows upright. The ash remembers every false claim—and every soul that endured it.
