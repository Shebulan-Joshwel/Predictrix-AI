# The Ashreach Hearing: A Transcript Entered Under Seal

*[The chamber is roofed in blackened timber. Marsh-water ticks beneath the floorboards. At the far end, Maelis Harrowick the Pale stands beneath the torn device of the court. Their face is not recorded here. The clerk’s hand is unsteady.]*

**HERALD:** Let the hearing begin concerning Ashreach, and concerning Maelis Harrowick the Pale.

**CLERK:** Ashreach lies in **The Weeping Marshes**. Ashreach is **currently contested**. Its garrison numbers **2598**. It is therefore a defended but unsettled holding, rather than a core stronghold.

**PRESIDING VOICE:** Read the accusation.

**CLERK:** Maelis Harrowick the Pale, major **Court Chronicler**, born in **340 AS**, is accused of plotting the death of their own liege.

*[A stir passes through the chamber. From beyond the shutters comes the wet cry of something unseen.]*

**PRESIDING VOICE:** Maelis, do you deny the charge?

**MAELIS HARROWICK THE PALE:** I deny the comfort of the charge. Comfort is a poor instrument for truth.

**PRESIDING VOICE:** That is not an answer.

**MAELIS:** Then write this: I plotted what I believed had already been plotted against the realm.

**CLERK:** Shall that be entered as admission?

**MAELIS:** Enter what you heard. You are a Court Chronicler’s clerk, are you not? Do not mend the record merely because it limps.

**PRESIDING VOICE:** Did you plot the death of your own liege?

**MAELIS:** I set words in motion. Words are not knives, though knives often follow them.

**CLERK:** The accused does not deny setting words in motion.

*[A guard shifts. No name is given to the guard. The chain at their wrist gives a small iron sound.]*

**PRESIDING VOICE:** Why was Ashreach involved?

**MAELIS:** Because Ashreach is a wound left open in the marsh. Because its garrison is **2598**, and still the place is not secure. Because a holding may be defended and yet remain unsettled. Because every power that looks upon it sees an invitation.

**PRESIDING VOICE:** Did you command the garrison?

**MAELIS:** I was a chronicler. I commanded ink, when ink obeyed.

**CLERK:** The accused claims no command over Ashreach.

**PRESIDING VOICE:** You speak as though the court were the guilty party.

**MAELIS:** Courts prefer a clean culprit. Ashreach offers none. It presents ash and water, tension and threat. A place like that teaches every witness to lie before opening their mouth.

*[Silence. The marsh-water ticks louder.]*

**PRESIDING VOICE:** Final question. Are you loyal to your liege?

**MAELIS:** Loyalty is the name survivors give to the side that prevailed.

**CLERK:** The accused refuses a direct answer.

**PRESIDING VOICE:** Then let the refusal stand beside the charge.

*[The seal is pressed. The wax cracks before it cools.]*
