# **The Ash-Wall Ballad of Vharenford and Ederon Coldwater**

Sing low, ash-throats, sing low,  
Where the dead winds scrape the stone;  
For Vharenford keeps watch below,  
And the wastes have made it bone.

In **291 AS**, its first stones rose  
Beneath a sky of iron hue;  
The Ash Wastes taught its gates to close,  
And taught its bells what fear can do.

It is a **core location**, stern and scarred,  
A garrisoned hold against the grey;  
Its watchfires burn behind the guard,  
Its sentries measure night and day.

**3637** stand to keep the wall—  
So the tally-board and captains say.  
Three thousand six hundred thirty-seven, all,  
With dust in their teeth and none to pray.

Sing low, ash-throats, sing low,  
Let no bright tongue praise the town;  
Vharenford does not bend or bow,  
Nor lay its iron manner down.

Then speak of **Ederon Coldwater**,  
Whose name is carried, breath by breath;  
A **Sapper**, sworn to earth and water,  
Who reads the buried road to death.

Born in **391 AS**, so the record tells,  
Though records crack and ink runs thin;  
His name is struck where the hammer fell,  
His trade is marked, but not his skin.

No chronicler grants his face to song,  
No proven temper, fierce or mild;  
The ash may hide what the ledgers wrong,  
And silence keeps the Sapper’s child.

Yet sing him still, for a sapper’s hand  
Knows the weight beneath the ground;  
It finds the hollow under the land,  
And hears where the buried dark is bound.

Do not say he loved the fortress wall,  
Or that Vharenford knew his tread;  
The truthful page does not say it all,  
And truth is a nail in the coffin of dead.

Still, when the night wind claws the gate,  
When watchmen pale at the powder’s sigh,  
The singers join his name to the state  
Of the hard-held town beneath the sky.

“Ederon!” cries the ash-black road.  
“Vharenford!” answers the guarded stone.  
One bears a craft, one bears a load,  
And each stands grimly there alone.

Sing low, ash-throats, sing low,  
Let the cinders veil the morn;  
Where Vharenford keeps watch below,  
The Sapper’s song is never born—

Yet never wholly dies away,  
For walls remember what men omit;  
And dust will carry, day by day,  
The names that no bright histories fit.
