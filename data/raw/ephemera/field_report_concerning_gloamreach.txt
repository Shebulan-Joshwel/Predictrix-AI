# Field Report: On the Sealed Reach and the Crooked Vale

**Filed by:** a road-worn observer of the Ashen Vanguard  
**Condition of report:** written under poor light, with the coast still in my clothes

## I. Gloamreach

Gloamreach lies on the Pale Coast, though it does not seem to belong to the coast around it. It is a core location, held behind its own silence, and its garrison numbers **2,483**. I counted the standards where I could see them. Whether every bearer still lived beneath those colors, I cannot swear.

The place is quarantined. The word is painted, nailed, and spoken through shut doors. It sets Gloamreach apart from the surrounding coast more surely than any wall. Boats keep their distance. Travelers are watched. Even the gulls appear to turn aside, though that may be only the weather or my own unease.

Gloamreach bears the pallid, bleak character of the Pale Coast. Its stone seems leeched of warmth, and the roads carry the color of old ash. Yet the name gives it another face: a shadowed identity, as if the settlement were not merely darkened but concealing something beneath the dark. I found no welcome there, nor did I expect one.

Its demeanor is sealed-off and ominous. Every gate suggested a question. Every sentry held uncertainty behind the eyes. I was permitted to observe, not to understand. That distinction may be the whole purpose of the place.

## II. Crookvale

Crookvale was founded in **295 AS** in **The High Spires**. It has been rebuilt, and the rebuilding shows in every hard seam and uneven angle. The settlement retains a garrison of **1004**, though Crookvale remains outside the core holdings.

I was told the vale had survived what should have ended it. I believe the stones more than the speaker. They stand crooked, but not weak; sharp-edged, but not careless. The structure bears an identity suited to the highland settlement, where straight lines are perhaps a luxury and softness a liability.

Crookvale feels wary and resilient. Its people watched the road as if the road had betrayed them before. Doors closed without slamming. Hands remained near tools and weapons. Survival has become its governing principle—not a banner, not a prayer, but a habit worn into the settlement until no one remembers behaving otherwise.

## Closing Judgment

Gloamreach guards its uncertainty by sealing the world out. Crookvale endures by watching the world closely. I would not call either place safe. I would call both difficult to break.

That is not the same thing. But in this age, it may be the nearest thing left.
